1. Overview

This assignment asks for a plan to push the app to $10M ARR in six months. The document follows five parts: understand the pain point, research (own product and competitors, plus the payment gateway), product improvements, marketing improvements, and an ARR strategy built on expanding beyond India.

2. The Problem: What Users Struggle With

Based on my research, a gay person using a dating app can run into many edge cases:

  • No matches, or too many matches
  • No real people, and getting scammed
  • Unable to verify who is behind a profile
  • Nudity and inappropriate text, with no filtering in my testing
  • Limited chat accessibility (emojis, GIFs) and no voice messages
  • Compromised anonymity
  • A weak reporting feature
  • A boring app with "no spice"

These are issues that no app has fully solved yet. Many have come close.

Sources: Brno Daily · MDPI · PMC · GoodTherapy

3. Research: What to Remove

The app has 100K+ downloads on the Play Store. I assumed MAU at around 50% of that base, since the real number isn't available. To grow in six months, these are the things I would remove or change:

What to changeWhy
Instant paywallUsers who hit a payment requirement right after download often quit and uninstall. AppsFlyer · Business of Apps · RevenueCat
Android-onlyTo push toward $10M ARR with worldwide usage, iOS users must be included. World Population Review · GoodFirms · Tekrevol
Poor filteringCompetitors offering the same concept have more filtering options. ResearchGate · Wired
Razorpay onlyTo expand, the app needs gateways that work in every country and outside India, such as Stripe or Apple Pay.

4. Product Improvements

Reaching $10M ARR is only realistic if these changes are made, based on competitor research and a few interviews I conducted.

🎯 Core idea: a dual-app structure for two kinds of users

Dating and Curious. One group uses the app to date, while the other is there out of curiosity and a desire to learn more. On sign-up, ask users which group they belong to.

  • The Dating section promotes swiping, with real photos required.
  • The Curious section promotes commenting and conversation, and users can stay anonymous with no verification.
  • Both sections get their own subscription module.

Feature list

(A screenshot-blocking feature already exists, so I did not include it.)

5. Marketing Strategy

Marketing has to be careful. Indian society is not yet comfortable for gay people, especially in Tier 2 and 3 cities. There are two target audiences: people looking for dating (serious or for fun), and curious people.

  • Partner influencers for paid promotions
  • In-app branded content
  • Showcase safety and trust
  • Highlight affordable subscription tiers and the one-profile option
  • Showcase events, including virtual speed-dating hosted by us
  • SEO-optimised content
  • Paid ads across platforms in India and other countries
  • Partnerships with LGBTQ community NGOs

6. ARR Strategy

Reaching the target requires expanding beyond India. Assuming a move to the USA, where LGBTQ is widely supported, the platform can be set up there. There will be many edge cases in user psychology and behaviour, so nothing should be predicted without A/B testing first.

The path to $10M ARR:

  1. Improve the product
  2. Understand user behaviour toward our app and other apps
  3. Market and promote it well
