Introduction

The Pluxee card, earlier known as the Sodexo meal card, is a prepaid meal-benefit card that many Indian companies give to their employees as part of their salary structure. The card runs on Zeta's payments and card-processing platform. Pluxee says it works with 11,000+ companies in India and reaches 3.5+ million consumers(Guesstimated), with 1,80,000+ meal merchant acceptance points across 1,800+ towns Guesstimated).

Employers load a monthly food allowance onto the card, and a lot of employees spend it online, on food delivery and quick-commerce apps like Swiggy, Zomato, Zepto, Blinkit and Instamart. The allowance is treated as a tax-friendly benefit (subject to the applicable tax rules), so it's worth having. The catch is that it can only be spent on food and non-alcoholic beverages, and only with merchants registered under food-related categories.

This case study looks only at the online side of the problem: what happens when a Pluxee cardholder tries to check out on an app or website.

The Challenge

The card works, until the cart has something other than food in it

Pluxee cardholders often struggle to pay for online orders that mix food with non-food items. The card is built for eligible food and meal purchases, so anything outside that list gets blocked. People end up with money on the card they can't easily use, and a checkout experience that feels like a puzzle every time.

Here's how the problem breaks down:

  1. Restrictions on payment categories: The card is meant for eligible food and meal purchases. Non-food items are generally not allowed.
  2. Online payment issues: On e-commerce and quick-commerce apps, Pluxee can't be used when the order includes non-food items or when the merchant falls outside the allowed categories.
  3. Limited flexibility in spending: Users can't use their balance for other everyday purchases, even when a good amount is sitting unused.
  4. Complicated checkout: Users have to remove items from their cart or switch to a different payment method to get the order through.
  5. Unused monthly balance: Because of all this, a part of the monthly meal benefit often goes unspent.
🎯

Core user problem: Pluxee cardholders don't get a smooth, flexible online payment experience. Restrictions on eligible transactions stop them from using their balance when an order includes non-food items.

How Pluxee works on apps today

Before designing anything, we wanted to understand where the "food only" rule is actually enforced. From Pluxee's own help pages and what users report, it works at two levels:

LevelWhat decides itWhat the user sees
Which apps can accept PluxeeThe merchant's category code (MCC). Only food-related categories are allowed.Pluxee simply isn't available on apps outside these categories.
Which carts can be paid with PluxeeThe merchant's own checkout, which knows what's in the cart. Pluxee is offered as its own payment option. On Zepto, for example, users add the card under the Pluxee section of the payment options.Users report that on apps like Instamart, if even one non-food item is in the cart, Pluxee doesn't show up as a payment option at all.

This is an important detail. The card network never sees the toothpaste in Rohan's cart. It's the merchant's checkout that hides Pluxee when the cart isn't fully food. That's good news for us, because the same checkout already has everything it needs (item details, categories, prices) to split the cart instead of blocking it.

This is our reading of public information and user reports. It needs to be confirmed with Pluxee and its merchant partners.

Why some merchants can't accept Pluxee at all

Pluxee transactions only go through with merchants registered under food and meal categories. Based on our research, these are the MCCs that are accepted:

The MCC describes the merchant, not the product. An app can sell rice, snacks and milk, but if its payment setup is registered under a different primary business category, Pluxee won't work there. That's a separate problem from mixed carts, and as we explain in the Scope section, it's not the one we're solving here.

Does a split option already exist?

In public sources and user reports, we couldn't find any major app that automatically splits one order between Pluxee and UPI or a card. The closest thing is the wallet top-up trick above. That gap is what this case study goes after.

Apps change their checkouts often, so this should be rechecked directly in each app before the case study is presented.

What users are actually saying

We went through user discussions on Reddit and Grapevine to see how this plays out in real life. One Reddit post stood out. A user gets ₹8,000 a month on their Pluxee card under the new tax rules and now has ₹20,000+ stuck on the card. Quick-commerce apps accept it, but the user finds eligible items like protein far more expensive there, so they don't want to spend it there either.

The replies, and other threads, said a lot too:

  • Blinkit and Instamart reportedly accept Pluxee for eligible purchases, but some users say it doesn't work for them at all.
  • Whey protein may go through as food, depending on how it's classified. Supplements like omega capsules may not.
  • Users report that a single non-food item in the cart makes the Pluxee option disappear.

What we took away from this:

  • Users can build up large unused balances over a few months.
  • What counts as "food" isn't always obvious, and users can't predict whether Pluxee will be available.
  • Mixed carts (food plus non-food) are where most of the friction shows up.
  • Some frustrations, like higher prices on certain apps, have nothing to do with payments. A checkout fix won't solve those.

  • He wants to use up his full monthly allowance instead of letting it pile up.
  • He likes ordering from apps that are convenient for him, like Instamart and Blinkit. He doesn't want to change his habits just to suit the card.

Rohan's problem

Rohan has trouble using his Pluxee card when he shops online. Not every app accepts it, and even on the ones that do, Pluxee disappears from the payment options the moment his cart has a non-food item.

Say Rohan places his weekly order on Instamart. Along with the groceries he adds a dishwashing liquid, a pack of tissues and some toothpaste. At checkout, he can't pay with Pluxee. Now he has two options. He can take out the non-food items, or pay for the whole thing with his UPI or debit card. Either way, a 2-minute checkout turns into a mess, and his Pluxee balance stays unused.

The Objective and Scope

Let users spend their meal benefit without fighting the checkout

✅

Final objective: Build a seamless online checkout experience that helps Pluxee cardholders make full use of their eligible meal benefits, while reducing payment friction when they shop on apps and websites.

In practice, that means:

  • The cart is sorted into food and non-food automatically, so users never have to remove items themselves.
  • Users go through one checkout, not two orders.
  • The food part is paid from Pluxee (approved with an OTP) and the rest with UPI, a debit card, a credit card or another method.
  • Users always know what was paid from where, and why.
  • More of the monthly balance actually gets used.

What's in scope, and what isn't

In scope

  • Mixed carts on apps that already accept Pluxee
  • Splitting one order between Pluxee and one other payment method
  • Refunds, returns and failures for split orders

Out of scope

  • Apps whose merchant category doesn't allow Pluxee. That's a merchant onboarding problem, not a checkout one.
  • Prices on apps that accept Pluxee. Users finding items expensive is a pricing issue that split payments won't fix.
  • Spending Pluxee on non-food items. That would break the rules the benefit depends on.

Stakeholders and Business Case

Who builds it

Zeta builds it, Pluxee offers it, and merchants plug it in. Zeta already runs Pluxee's card processing, so it's the natural owner of the two new pieces this needs: an eligibility service that decides which items count as food, and a split payment orchestrator that runs the two payments as one order. Pluxee offers this to its merchant partners as part of its acceptance, and each merchant integrates it once through an API or SDK. The UPI or card part still runs through the merchant's existing payment gateway, so no new money-handling setup is needed.

[Why Pluxee should own the split and not leave it to merchants: merchants already tag food and non-food items, so sharing that data is the easy part. But the rule for what the card may pay for is a compliance and tax-exemption question, and the risk sits with Pluxee, not the merchant. Merchants also gain from loosening the rule, and each one has its own category setup. One Pluxee-owned eligibility list gives a single, auditable rule for every app, and Pluxee can change it without waiting on merchants. See the note at the end of this page.]

Why everyone has a reason to say yes

StakeholderWhat they getWhat they give
Cardholders (Rohan)One checkout, no removed items, and a meal balance that actually gets usedOne extra approval step (OTP for Pluxee)
Merchants (Instamart, Zepto, Blinkit)Fewer abandoned carts, bigger baskets, and Pluxee users who don't need to place two ordersA one-time integration and support for split refunds

Who pays for it

  • Users: nothing extra.
  • Merchants: standard Pluxee fees on the Pluxee part, and their usual gateway fees on the rest. No extra fee during the pilot.
  • Pluxee and Zeta: share the build cost. After the pilot, a small per-transaction platform fee from Pluxee to Zeta is one option to consider.

The pricing model is a proposal for discussion, not something Pluxee or Zeta have announced.

The Solution

Split payments for online checkout

The idea is simple. Instead of hiding Pluxee when the cart isn't fully food, the checkout splits the order behind the scenes and pays each part with the right method, all in one flow.

💡

In one line: Food items get paid from Pluxee, everything else gets paid with UPI or a card, and the user places one order without removing a single item.

How it works

  1. Automatic cart categorisation: At checkout, the system sorts the cart into food and non-food items on its own.
  2. Single checkout experience: The user sees one checkout flow. There's no need to split the purchase manually or place two orders.
  3. Backend split payment: The food portion is charged to the Pluxee card and the non-food portion to another payment method.
  4. Pluxee authentication: The user enters an OTP to approve the Pluxee part of the payment.
  5. Alternative payment method: The non-food items are paid with UPI, a debit card, a credit card or any other supported method.
  6. Seamless checkout: Both payments are handled inside the same checkout, so the user doesn't have to remove items or place separate orders.

A few design choices worth calling out:

  • The split is shown before the user taps Pay, so nothing comes as a surprise.
  • Pluxee goes first. It's the payment most likely to fail (OTP, balance), so we get it out of the way before touching the user's UPI or card.
  • A clear progress tracker shows both steps, so two approvals feel like one flow.
  • A reassurance line tells the user that if the second payment fails, the Pluxee amount is released automatically.

How it's built, in short

The merchant's checkout sends the cart to Zeta's eligibility service, which tags each item as food or non-food. Zeta's split payment orchestrator then runs the Pluxee payment first (approve and hold), takes the UPI or card payment through the merchant's gateway, and only then confirms the Pluxee amount. If anything fails, the other payment is reversed automatically. The full architecture, payment sequence and reliability rules are in the Technical Appendix (chapter 15).

Risks and Trade-offs

Risks

ComplianceIf split orders are seen as misuse of a tax-exempt benefit, the whole product is at riskOnly food ever touches Pluxee, every order keeps an item-level audit trail, and legal review happens before the pilot

Engineering complexityTwo payments in one order means more failure pointsClear order states, idempotency, reconciliation, and a slow, flagged rollout

Trade-offs we made on purpose

  • Two payment methods, not more. Supporting three or more would cover rare cases but make the flow and the refunds much harder.
  • Unknown items count as non-food. Users might sometimes pay a little more from UPI than they'd like, but we never risk the benefit's tax status.
  • Pluxee first, UPI second. This adds a small delay before the UPI step, but it avoids taking money from a user's bank account and then refunding it.
  • One pilot partner, not all apps at once. It's slower to reach everyone, but much safer to learn on.

Key Takeaways

  • The rule isn't the problem, the checkout is. The food-only rule makes sense. What hurts users is that a single non-food item makes Pluxee disappear.
  • The merchant already has what it needs. The checkout knows every item in the cart, so it can split the order instead of blocking it. [The merchant supplies the item data, but Pluxee should own the eligibility rule because it carries the compliance risk and needs one consistent, auditable rule across every merchant.]
  • Reliability matters more than features. Users will forgive one extra step. They won't forgive paying for half an order.
  • Compliance is part of the design. Only food touches Pluxee, and every split leaves an audit trail.
  • Start small and prove it. One partner, clear targets, then scale.

Technical Appendix

This section explains how the split checkout would actually be built. It's a proposed design, not how Pluxee or Zeta work today.

The building blocks

ComponentWho owns itWhat it does
Merchant checkout (app or website)Merchant, e.g. Zepto or InstamartShows the split to the user, collects the OTP and the second payment, and sends the full cart with item details to Zeta.
Eligibility serviceZeta (rules owned by Pluxee)Keeps the approved list of food categories and SKUs. Takes a cart and returns which items are eligible and the eligible total.
Split payment orchestratorZetaCreates one parent order with two child payments, runs them in the right order, and rolls back if something fails.
Pluxee card processingZetaChecks the balance, sends the OTP, authorises and captures the food amount on the Pluxee card.
Secondary payment gatewayMerchant's existing gatewayHandles UPI, debit and credit cards and other methods for the non-food amount.
Ledger and reconciliationZeta + merchantRecords which items were paid by which method, so refunds, settlements and audits all match up.

Sorting the cart

Each item in the cart carries its SKU and the merchant's product category. The merchant sends the full cart to the eligibility service, which checks every line item against the approved list and sends back an eligible or not-eligible flag for each one.

  • The check runs on the server, never only in the app. The app can't be trusted to decide what's food, so the server recalculates the split before any money moves.
  • Unknown items default to non-food. If an item has no category or isn't on the list, it goes to the second payment method.
  • Fees follow a fixed rule. Delivery fees, platform fees, packaging and tips are tagged non-food by default unless the rules say otherwise.
  • Discounts are spread proportionally. A cart-level coupon is divided between the food and non-food portions based on their share of the cart.
  • The eligibility list is versioned. Every split stores which version of the list it used, so it can be audited later.

Running the two payments

The two payments are treated as one transaction: either both go through, or neither does.

    O->>P: Capture food amount
    O-->>M: Order confirmed
    M-->>U: One order, two receipts

If the Pluxee card supports authorise now, capture later, the food amount is only held until the second payment succeeds, and released straight away if it fails. If it doesn't, the fallback is to charge it immediately and refund it automatically if the second payment fails.

Reliability rules

  • Idempotency keys: every payment request carries a unique key, so a retry after a network drop never charges the user twice.
  • Status checks before retries: if a payment times out, the orchestrator asks the gateway for the status before trying again.
  • Webhooks plus polling: payment results come in through webhooks, with a status check as a backup in case a webhook is late or lost.
  • Hold timeouts: if the second payment isn't finished within a set window (for example 10 minutes), the Pluxee hold is released automatically.
  • Item-level ledger: each line item is linked to the payment that covered it. Refunds, partial returns and reconciliation all read from this.
  • Two settlements, one order: Pluxee and the payment gateway settle with the merchant separately. A daily reconciliation job matches both back to the parent order.
  • Card data security: the merchant never stores card details. Card payments go through the gateway's tokenised flow, in line with PCI DSS and RBI card tokenisation rules.

Compliance checks

  • Only the food portion ever touches the Pluxee balance, so the meal benefit stays within the tax rules for food allowances.
  • The item-level data and eligibility-list version are stored with every transaction, which gives Pluxee and employers a clean audit trail.
  • The setup has to stay within RBI's rules for prepaid payment instruments, which Pluxee and Zeta would need to confirm before launch.
